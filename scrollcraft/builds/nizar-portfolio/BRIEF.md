Interviewed. Verbatim answers are quoted; everything marked **Authored** is a
decision made under the owner's delegation ("be creative", "leave them to your
imagination", "if you think of anything better go for it").

## The world: The Long Game (authored)

One place. An endless chessboard of black stone at the edge of Lake Ontario at
night. The pieces stand at the scale of buildings, and past the far rank the
board becomes Toronto: the CN Tower stands among the towers like one more piece.
Circuit traces are etched into the squares and carry warm light when a thought
passes through them (tech, AI). Fog, black water, a pine shoreline (nature).
Everything is cold blue-black except one warm ember light: the lit king, the
lit doors, the square that is waiting for a move.

Style preamble (verbatim on every generated asset):

> Cinematic night photography, 35mm anamorphic lens, shallow depth of field.
> Black polished stone chess pieces at architectural scale on a vast dark stone
> chessboard beside a still black lake, heavy low fog, distant city lights.
> One warm ember-orange practical light, cool blue-black ambient fill, deep
> falloff into shadow. Grade: blue-black shadows, bone-white highlights, a
> single ember accent. Wet reflective surfaces, fine film grain, slight
> halation. Photographic realism. No text, no logos, no neon purple, no
> cartoon, no clay, no plastic sheen.

Tokens: canvas `#07090C`, surface `#0F1319`, ink `#ECE7DF`, ink-soft `#98A0AB`,
accent `#FF8A4C` (ember), accent-ink `#140A04`. Display Archivo (expanded,
heavy), text Geist, notation Geist Mono (used only for chess notation and data).

## Grammar

**Continuous world** (uniqueness.md 2.4): one fixed stage for the whole landing
page, copy arriving at waypoints, no section seams. The nav is a map (the
scoresheet), the hero is an establishing position, the close is an arrival and
the CTA is an object in that place (the square waiting for the reply).

Declared deviation: the grammar asks for worldflight video legs. This build uses
one fixed real-time WebGL stage instead, because the doors must carry real
certificates and project covers, respond to the pointer and be clickable, and a
real-time camera cannot have a seam. Generated imagery feeds the world as
textures, plates, covers and posters.

Why the other seven lost: filmic one-shot forbids jumping, and a portfolio
visitor must be able to; chaptered editorial and typographic poster have no room
for a 3D world; live surface needs a product; gallery fits /projects (and is
used there) but not the argument of the landing page; split stage has no second
side; a cutlist cannot hold a peak this long.

## Revision 2 (dark branch): the scene rethought

Owner's verdict on revision 1: "I like the concept and the flow but everything
is super finicky and doesnt look polished. The light effects are way too bright
and scroll is inconsistently fast at wrong places! The entire concept of lake
and toronto city looks really bad!" Asked what to do with it, he chose "Rethink
the scene" and added: "doesnt need to be all dark but i want cleaner premium
design". Earlier steer, same owner: "glass, blue lights, marble, clouds,
architecture and all clean aesthetics", light "in closed space but wider and
longer field of view", "illuminating animated wavy lights".

Kept: the concept, the six moves, the copy, the scoresheet, the tokens, the
real-time stage. **Authored** from here down.

**The world now.** One long gallery hall at blue hour, above the cloud. The
floor is the board (Carrara and Marquina squares, a brass hairline between
them) and runs the whole length. One wall is pale stone, the other is glass
between slender columns with the dusk sky outside. The pieces stand on the
floor as sculpture. One thread of warm light runs overhead from the first
square, through a dark threshold, up the middle of the tower and ends under the
last board. Toronto is one more piece: a CN Tower in black and white marble on
a plinth by the glass. The tower of frames stands in a pale stone atrium open
to the sky; the last board floats above its rim on a sea of cloud.

**Rules of the rebuild** (each answers a complaint):
- Nothing in the world pops, rises, swings or fades. Only the camera, the
  thread and the cloud move. (finicky, glitching)
- No bloom, no glow sprites, no point light on a picture. Pictures are evenly
  lit panels in a dark bronze slab; the floor is a soft planar mirror; tone
  mapping is AgX. (too bright, low-quality frames)
- The camera is one spline with a monotone pacing curve. Dwell while copy is
  read, travel sized to distance: measured 6 to 21 units per viewport of scroll
  on every leg, no stop-go at the joins. The act is 15 viewports; the tower
  still has the largest span. (inconsistent scroll speed)
- The lake, the procedural skyline, the pines, the embers, the circuit traces
  and the figure cutout are gone. (lake and Toronto)
- Textures are procedural and uploaded before first sight; tower images are
  downscaled once. (glitches at the tower)

Feeling curve, unchanged in intent: curiosity (the hall, his name on the stone
wall), intimacy (among the pieces against the sky), appetite (three lit
panels), trust (the home piece), held breath (the dark threshold, which is now
architecture instead of fog), awe (the climb), resolve (the board above the
cloud). Peak: the climb round the tower.

### Revision 6: no repeats between pages; the experience record reads at a glance
Owner: "informatiion is repeated between about and experience page. The pieces i play with goes to experience section and
removed from about section, remove the stats from about page . certificates also go to experience, the hero for experience
page is not smooth and forces user to stop scroll for each sentence . rethink that and come up with new concept. Also
remove the small description under the title in about section. For experience keep a good balance between animations and
redability with the option to read all at a glance always available."
- About is the person only: title page (portrait, king, two links), the story chapters, the live training numbers. The
  numbers band, the paragraph under the title, the piece picker and the credentials are gone from it.
- Experience is everything professional: the record, the piece picker, the degrees and every certificate (#certificates).
- New concept for the record: the ledger and the board side by side, nothing pinned. Every result is written out in the
  ledger, and all of them are named at a glance at the top (one tap goes to the line). The board stays in view beside the
  ledger (sticky): its columns rise once by themselves; after that the result being read, or pointed at on either side,
  lights on the board and in the ledger, and the camera turns toward it with a one-second damped glide. The scroll is never
  held. On a phone the board shows once as an overview between the glance and the ledger. Reduced motion: no board, the
  ledger at full width.

### Revision 7: Experience rethought as one world with a place for each part of the record
Owner: "bad ! Rethink the entire experience again". What was wrong, in the owner's words: "glitching description, feels
diconnected, boring animations, no character". Direction: "all three ideas are great! maybe combining three would work"
(one world behind the page, a place per company with its own visual, a sideways gallery walk).
- Grammar: one world held behind the whole page (fixed WebGL canvas), the page itself ordinary sections in ordinary flow
  over it. Nothing pinned except short sticky copy; the camera glides between places on a Catmull-Rom route keyed to s
  (one unit per section), damped, never stopping.
- Places: the start (the landing's black king on its plinth, a pawn and a knight in the mist); CGI as a sideways gallery
  walk along a marble runway, each result a column as tall as its figure, rising as it comes in from the right, with its
  words riding beside it (never swapped in place); Fiera as the migration, twenty dark server blocks lifting one by one off
  an on-premise rack in an arc onto a deck held up by a marble cloud, while the pinned figures count up with them; the
  stack as five pieces on plinths (pick one: it lifts and turns, its name sits under it, the detail below), carried away
  with its section; the degrees and certificates on the plain ground as the world steps back.
- Reading at a glance: "All results" is fixed in the corner on every screen and opens every result in full; a fixed index
  names each place and jumps to where the camera has arrived. Both come first in the reading order.
- Phone: its own framing per place (the walk closer with the passing column centred and its words over it; the migration
  further back below pinned figures, the sentences following on a card that slides up over them; the pieces further back
  above their names). Reduced motion or no WebGL: no world, the same sections as plain lists.
- Feel check (cold, desktop): recognised (the king) / absorbed (columns rising and passing, words travelling with them) /
