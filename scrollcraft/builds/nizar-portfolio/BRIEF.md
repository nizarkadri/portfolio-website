# BRIEF: Nizar Kadri portfolio

Interviewed. Verbatim answers are quoted; everything marked **Authored** is a
decision made under the owner's delegation ("be creative", "leave them to your
imagination", "if you think of anything better go for it").

## The eight topics

1. **Vibe and references.** "dark", "a film". Supplied references: a tower of
   floating doors and frames above a lone figure, a black chess king glowing in
   fog on a floating board, a sculpted pastel relief with a warm neon seam, a
   white spotlit podium. **Authored:** cold, quiet, nocturnal, deliberate, one
   warm light.
2. **Journey.** "Landing page must be shockingly impressive ... inspiration from
   chess (I have been playing chess since childhood and I am really good at it,
   I wanna make user feel the connection of intelligence in chess and coding and
   designing and solving problems). The next a glance to my projects and
   experience which they can explore more by navigating to dedicated pages. The
   next would be the about section which I would keep separate from landing
   page. All from start to end the user should feel the urge to reach out to me."
3. **Energy curve.** **Authored:** quiet open, rising through the work, a held
   silence, the loudest moment at the tower, then stillness at the close.
4. **Feeling and the one moment.** "The tower ... if you think of anything
   better than tower go for it."
5. **What no other site does.** **Authored:** the page is a chess game the
   visitor plays by scrolling, and its last move is theirs.
6. **Distance from premium-minimal.** "more premium", "dark". **Authored:**
   premium cinematic nocturne, not minimal: a full 3D world, dense atmosphere.
7. **One world or scenes.** "connected worlds but the core theme revolves around
   tech, ai, chess, toronto and a bit of nature, it would be really impressive
   if you can come up with a world that involves it all".
8. **Assets.** Four reference images (inspiration only, recreated, not shipped).
   Four LinkedIn certificate PDFs now, "total 15 certificates which I will add
   later" (placeholders). Portraits: "choose one or two max". Higgsfield
   generation authorised mid-build; kie.ai is never called.

Scope: "Entire website". Project library: "3d scrollable animations with each
project having a title tech stack and if available a live preview". Project
covers: "more polished covers which you need to generate".

## Who, belief, action

- **What / who for:** the portfolio of Nizar Kadri, full stack developer in

  recruiters, engineering leads and clients.
- **Belief to install:** this person thinks several moves ahead, and builds the
  way he plays.
- **One action, one label:** `Make your move` goes to /contact. Used everywhere.

## The world: The Long Game (authored)

One place. An endless chessboard of black stone at the edge of Lake Ontario at
night. The pieces stand at the scale of buildings, and past the far rank the
board becomes Toronto: the CN Tower stands among the towers like one more piece.
Circuit traces are etched into the squares and carry warm light when a thought
passes through them (tech, AI). Fog, black water, a pine shoreline (nature).
Everything is cold blue-black except one warm ember light: the lit king, the
lit doors, the square that is waiting for a move.

Style preamble (verbatim on every generated asset):

> Cinematic night photography, 35mm anamorphic lens, shallow depth of field.
> Black polished stone chess pieces at architectural scale on a vast dark stone
> chessboard beside a still black lake, heavy low fog, distant city lights.
> One warm ember-orange practical light, cool blue-black ambient fill, deep
> falloff into shadow. Grade: blue-black shadows, bone-white highlights, a
> single ember accent. Wet reflective surfaces, fine film grain, slight
> halation. Photographic realism. No text, no logos, no neon purple, no
> cartoon, no clay, no plastic sheen.

Tokens: canvas `#07090C`, surface `#0F1319`, ink `#ECE7DF`, ink-soft `#98A0AB`,
accent `#FF8A4C` (ember), accent-ink `#140A04`. Display Archivo (expanded,
heavy), text Geist, notation Geist Mono (used only for chess notation and data).

## Grammar

**Continuous world** (uniqueness.md 2.4): one fixed stage for the whole landing
page, copy arriving at waypoints, no section seams. The nav is a map (the
scoresheet), the hero is an establishing position, the close is an arrival and
the CTA is an object in that place (the square waiting for the reply).

Declared deviation: the grammar asks for worldflight video legs. This build uses
one fixed real-time WebGL stage instead, because the doors must carry real
certificates and project covers, respond to the pointer and be clickable, and a
real-time camera cannot have a seam. Generated imagery feeds the world as
textures, plates, covers and posters.

Why the other seven lost: filmic one-shot forbids jumping, and a portfolio
visitor must be able to; chaptered editorial and typographic poster have no room
for a 3D world; live surface needs a product; gallery fits /projects (and is
used there) but not the argument of the landing page; split stage has no second
side; a cutlist cannot hold a peak this long.

**Peak:** move 5, the largest span on the page. Said to a friend: "the fog
closed, then a tower of glowing doors climbed out of a chessboard with him
standing under it."

**Tell-someone sentence:** it's the site where you scroll through a chess game
played across Toronto at night, a tower of doors rises out of the board, and
the last move is yours.

**Signature move: the scoresheet.** A persistent score of the game in chess
notation. Scrolling writes it: each waypoint passed stamps its move and stays.

visitor plays the reply; that move is carried to /contact, where the form is the
reply.

## Revision 2 (dark branch): the scene rethought

Owner's verdict on revision 1: "I like the concept and the flow but everything
is super finicky and doesnt look polished. The light effects are way too bright
and scroll is inconsistently fast at wrong places! The entire concept of lake
and toronto city looks really bad!" Asked what to do with it, he chose "Rethink
the scene" and added: "doesnt need to be all dark but i want cleaner premium
design". Earlier steer, same owner: "glass, blue lights, marble, clouds,
architecture and all clean aesthetics", light "in closed space but wider and
longer field of view", "illuminating animated wavy lights".

Kept: the concept, the six moves, the copy, the scoresheet, the tokens, the
real-time stage. **Authored** from here down.

**The world now.** One long gallery hall at blue hour, above the cloud. The
floor is the board (Carrara and Marquina squares, a brass hairline between
them) and runs the whole length. One wall is pale stone, the other is glass
between slender columns with the dusk sky outside. The pieces stand on the
floor as sculpture. One thread of warm light runs overhead from the first
square, through a dark threshold, up the middle of the tower and ends under the
last board. Toronto is one more piece: a CN Tower in black and white marble on
a plinth by the glass. The tower of frames stands in a pale stone atrium open
to the sky; the last board floats above its rim on a sea of cloud.

**Rules of the rebuild** (each answers a complaint):
- Nothing in the world pops, rises, swings or fades. Only the camera, the
  thread and the cloud move. (finicky, glitching)
- No bloom, no glow sprites, no point light on a picture. Pictures are evenly
  lit panels in a dark bronze slab; the floor is a soft planar mirror; tone
  mapping is AgX. (too bright, low-quality frames)
- The camera is one spline with a monotone pacing curve. Dwell while copy is
  read, travel sized to distance: measured 6 to 21 units per viewport of scroll
  on every leg, no stop-go at the joins. The act is 15 viewports; the tower
  still has the largest span. (inconsistent scroll speed)
- The lake, the procedural skyline, the pines, the embers, the circuit traces
  and the figure cutout are gone. (lake and Toronto)
- Textures are procedural and uploaded before first sight; tower images are
  downscaled once. (glitches at the tower)

Feeling curve, unchanged in intent: curiosity (the hall, his name on the stone
wall), intimacy (among the pieces against the sky), appetite (three lit
panels), trust (the home piece), held breath (the dark threshold, which is now
architecture instead of fog), awe (the climb), resolve (the board above the
cloud). Peak: the climb round the tower.

## Revision 3 (same branch): clean, pale, one subject

The owner sent two references (a white page with one black object and light
grotesk type; a pale blue editorial portrait with a serif headline) and said:
"this is what i mean by clean design. The navigation looks too crowded in your
version. The color is just bad i dont like orange, The landing page font and
style is bad. The main portrait needs to be restyled with higgsfield to have
better polished version of me". Then: "I like the direction you are going with
font styles, but i need more animations and effects in text instead of being
static. I want lively feel from the website and text", and of the portrait: "I
want only very subtle beard and moustache", "the face is not accurate" (of a
pass edited from a generated image rather than from his photo).

**Authored** in answer:
- Tokens on the landing page: canvas `#e3f2fb` (sampled from the portrait's
  backdrop), ink `#0b0f15`, ink-soft `#55606e`, accent = ink (black pills).
  No orange anywhere; the site accent on the dark pages is ice blue `#a9c8ec`.
- Type: Geist for display at weight 300 to 400, tight tracking. Archivo is gone.
- The bar: name, one pill of four links, one button. The scoresheet is six dots
  in the right margin with the current move set vertically under them.
- Hero: his portrait (Higgsfield edit of his own photo, pale blue studio, dark
  jacket) fills the right of the first screen and dissolves into the board as
  the scroll begins. One portrait on this page.
- The world lost its walls: a pale studio, the board running into mist, black
  and white pieces, a blue thread, white cloud under the last board. The
  silence before the tower is mist closing, as in revision 1.
- Type moves: the name rises letter by letter and its letters gain weight near
  the pointer; headings arrive word by word under the scroll (engine kinetic);
  code notes type themselves; the tower's numbers count up; ledger rows, door
  cards and buttons follow their heading in. Only the eyebrow dot and the caret
  repeat. All of it is off under reduced motion.

After the owner approved the portrait and asked for Work and About next, the
pale tokens moved to `:root`, so every page stands on the same ground.
- Work, second attempt. A stepped hall of doors was built first and the owner
  rejected it: "rethink the projects entirely, drop the idea of cards floating,
  its very glitchy and doesnt look good". The page is now flat (grammar: working
  surface). A large typographic list of every project; on a wide screen one
  steady panel beside it shows the project under the pointer, or the one at the
  reading line, with the new picture wiped over the old. Each line is the link.
  Search, order and stack filters act on the same list. On a phone there is no
  panel: each project carries its own picture, label and links. No 3D, no pin.
- Work, third attempt. He then sent two references (a cave lake lit from an
  opening overhead with a round podium of stone on glowing ice; a matte dark
  studio with planets in a row over a round platform and a small figure) and
  asked: "combine these assets and make a 3d world for projects, dont make it
  too dark or too bright keep the matte finish while having smooth transitions
  and animations". The top of the page is now one real WebGL scene
  (`components/work/Orrery.ts`): a still lake in a misty mid-tone cave, soft
  cones of light, the podium with one small king on it, and each project a
  matte planet (its cover wrapped round it) on a line behind. The project you
  are on rises over the podium, ringed. Every position is a pure function of
  one number, so transitions glide and cannot pop. Scroll is stepped (rest on
  each project for two thirds of its scroll). The label is flat markup beside
  the canvas. The flat list stays below it as the index. No bloom, roughness
  near 1 on everything, PCF soft shadows.
  He then asked for more of the cave picture: "the cyan blue lake behind podium
  and light rays and blue crystal glow under the podium". The podium now stands
  on a shore with a luminous cyan lake curving round behind it; the roof has a
  visible opening with a fan of eight rays that land in pools of light; the
  base is a faceted crystal lit from inside, with a lamp that lights the shore.
  Then: "its perfect but flat, i want a 3d world and higher saturation of blue,
  if possible instead of king piece bring the globes down a little but so they
  are floating directly above podium with blue glow and edge the tech stack
  around the lower glowing area". So: the globes ride a ring that runs away
  behind the podium and comes forward at both sides (no flat row); low ambient
  with one key and a blue light from the opening, so round things have a lit
  side; three depth layers (dark stone close to the camera, boulders at the
  water, walls, roof and stalactites behind); the camera leans into each change
  of project and back. Blues are saturated (air #6f90b6, lake and crystal
  cyan-blue). The king is gone; the active globe hangs just over the slab in a
  pool of blue light; the stack is drawn on a band round the front of the
  crystal and the HTML chips are hidden on wide screens.
  Then: "fix the scroll sync so projects come in and leave smoothly as user
  scroll, right now its way out of sync". The stepped mapping (rest for two
  thirds, switch in a burst) and the timed label swap are gone. Scroll now
  scrubs the scene: place = project index as a continuous function of scroll
  with a mild ease on arrival (never a stop). The scene has no loop or
  smoothing of its own; the page draws it once per frame from the scroll
  position and sets the label (opacity and offset) from the same number.
  Measured: 0 projects of drift, forwards and back.
  Then: "its in sync for the world part but the list moves too fast". On a
  wide screen the list now pins like the world and the scroll moves it one row
  at a time (34svh of scroll per project, about 270px on a laptop, against
  about 75px before). The row on the reading line is the active one; a resting
  pointer overrides it. The active row steps forward by transform only, so the
  list never reflows under the scroll. Phones and reduced motion keep the
  plain flowing list.
- Case studies (`/projects/[slug]`). The owner: "the project pages are absolutely
  unreadable". They still carried hard-coded dark Tailwind classes (white text,
  dark panels, orange labels) on the pale ground. Rebuilt with tokens in
  `app/case-study.css`: breadcrumb, tags, a light headline whose letters rise
  in, the lede, buttons and stack, the cover in a rounded frame, a row of facts,
  the write-up beside a white highlights panel, and previous and next cards.
  `ProjectBadges` and `ProjectLinks` use the same classes.
- About: a portrait card (the same Higgsfield portrait, no second generation),
  a headline whose letters rise in, three pills, a band of four numbers taken
  from the work, white certificate frames. The figure cut-out is gone.
- Experience, Blog and Contact inherit the palette and type; they were checked
  for breakage, not redesigned.

The project covers are still the ember set from revision 1.

Not done in this revision: a phone-specific composition pass, the Work and
About pages, generated assets (Higgsfield unlimited generations are refused
from Claude Code; they work from Claude chat).
